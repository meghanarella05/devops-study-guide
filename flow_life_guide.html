Your peak performance life guide

Based on the Flow Research Collective curriculum — use it every day

Wake up

Morning anchor

Set a consistent wake time. Before touching your phone, define your single highest-leverage task for today. Hydrate immediately (one full glass).

First 90 min

Deep work block (peak performance window)

Your first alert hours are your biological prime time. Block this for your hardest, most important work — no email, no notifications. This is your flow window.

Mid-morning

Movement + release

15–30 min walk or light movement. This is your "release trigger" — the transition out of struggle into recovery, letting insights incubate.

Midday

Shallow work + connections

Email, admin, meetings, communication. Protect your morning for deep work — reactive tasks belong here. Eat high protein, low sugar to avoid afternoon slump.

Afternoon

Second deep work block (optional)

A second focused work session if needed. Followed by a parasympathetic breath reset: 4 sec inhale → 4 hold → 6 exhale → 4 hold. Repeat 10×.

Evening

Wind-down protocol

No screens 60 min before bed. Review tomorrow's single highest-leverage task. Practice gratitude — 3 specific things. Keep the bedroom cold and dark.

Sleep

Non-negotiable (8 hrs)

Sleep is the most impactful performance variable — above nutrition and exercise. Guard it ruthlessly. It compounds everything else on this list.

Daily checklist — burnout-proofing basics
  • Prioritized sleep (8 hrs target)
  • Deep work block protected (no interruptions)
  • Walked 10,000+ steps
  • Drank water consistently (aim for pale yellow)
  • Ate whole foods, avoided hyper-palatable junk
  • Said no to at least one low-leverage request
  • Practiced gratitude or mindfulness (even 5 min)
  • Meaningful time with someone I care about
What flow is

Flow is the state of optimal experience — complete absorption in a challenging task where time distorts, self-consciousness disappears, and performance peaks.

The flow cycle — 4 stages

1. Struggle

Load your mind with the problem. Push past resistance. This stage is uncomfortable by design.

2. Release

Step away. Stop fighting. Let go of conscious control so insight can emerge.

3. Flow

Deep absorption begins. Performance and creativity peak. Protect this state.

4. Recovery

Replenish neurochemicals. Integrate learning. Prepare for the next cycle.

Flow triggers — use these to enter flow faster

Challenge / skill ratio

Task must be 4–10% harder than your current skill level. Too easy = boredom. Too hard = anxiety.

Deep embodiment

Full sensory engagement. Move your body, use your hands. Physical awareness accelerates flow.

Autonomy

Choose HOW you do the work. Control over method amplifies intrinsic motivation and flow.

Clear, immediate feedback

Know instantly if you're on track. Build feedback loops into every task.

The one-day month — your deep work superweapon
  • Schedule 1× per month as a repeating calendar event
  • Day before: define crystal-clear goals and map tasks to calendar
  • Guard your sleep the night before (AC + eye mask)
  • Block all technology and alert everyone you're going dark
  • Automate every routine decision for that day (meals, clothes, etc.)
The 5 forms of grit — train one per month

Grit to persevere

Stay on task through boredom, resistance, and setbacks. The backbone of long-term achievement.

Grit to control thoughts

Direct your mental narrative. Replace catastrophizing with curiosity and adaptive self-talk.

Best at your worst

Perform when tired, sick, or stressed. Build systems that work even when motivation fails.

Grit to master fear

Fear doesn't stop. You act anyway. Recognize and use fear as a signal, not a stop sign.

Grit to recover

Bounce back from failure, injury, or loss. Recovery is a skill, not just time passing.

Know yourself

Recognize your emotional triggers, strengths, weaknesses, and patterns before they run you.

Empathy

Understand others' perspectives and emotional states. The foundation of collaboration and leadership.

Overwhelm assassination — 10-step protocol
  • Qualify urgency — is this truly urgent or just loud?
  • Blanket ban everything non-essential to a fixed future date
  • Process linearly — one thing at a time, completely
  • Go dark — remove yourself from the stimulus
  • Change your explanatory style (this is temporary, not permanent)
  • Reduce cognitive load — eliminate, simplify, automate
  • Cut losses with cutthroat prioritization (ruthless triage)
  • Accept small sacrifices — let some things drop
  • Avoid meta-stress — radical acceptance of what you can't control
  • Take a walk, breathe — walks, breaks, and breath resets
The 80/20 of health — in priority order

Sleep

The single highest-leverage health variable. Everything else depends on this. 8 hrs, consistent schedule, cold dark room.

Nutrition

High satiety, low calorie density foods. Narrow your eating window. Avoid hyper-palatable processed food.

Exercise

150 min moderate or 75 min vigorous aerobic per week. Daily movement practice. Walk 10k steps minimum.

Psychological wellbeing

Mindfulness, gratitude, relationships, setting limits. This runs parallel to the other three and amplifies them.

Daily movement practice (5–10 min)
  • Forward bend into deep squat — breathe in/out slowly
  • Cat/cow spinal movement from yoga
  • Spinal and thoracic rotations left and right
  • 3 rounds of sun salutations — coordinating breath with movement
  • Foam rolling (hip flexors, thoracic spine, calves)
Hydration protocol
  • Always have a water bottle on hand — keep it full
  • Maintain 1:3 coffee-to-water ratio when drinking coffee
  • Rehydrate hard after exercise (electrolytes + water)
  • Aim for almost-clear urine color as target

Annual goals

Weekly and daily targets

Vision of dangerous — your definition of peak life

Define it concretely: how you feel internally each morning, how you engage with loved ones, what your environment contains, what work you do, what you're proud of, and what three words describe your lifestyle at its best.

Review this quarterly — ask yourself:
  • What 3 things am I most proud of accomplishing in the past 3 months?
  • What has gotten meaningfully easier for me?
  • What new goals have emerged?
  • Am I spending time in the ways this vision requires?
  • What are the 3 things I'm most excited about right now?
Identifying your strengths — the Buckingham test

A true strength meets all four criteria: you're great at it (not just good), anticipating it creates excitement, you drop into flow easily doing it, and performing it energizes rather than drains you.

Strengths assessment protocol
  • Take Angela Duckworth's Grit Scale assessment
  • Take StrengthsFinder 2.0 (by Tom Rath) — find your top 5
  • List activities where you consistently enter flow
  • Identify your "Buckingham strengths" — what energizes AND you excel at
  • Ask 3 people who know you well: "what do you see me doing at my best?"
  • Build your "baseball card" — a one-page profile of your strengths, tendencies, and weaknesses
The million dollar question

"How would you engage yourself daily if money was infinite?"

Your answer points directly to your latent leverage — what you're uniquely positioned to do in the world.

Amygdala bias warning

Your brain is evolutionarily wired to direct attention to weaknesses, not strengths. This is a protection mechanism — but it actively blocks peak performance. Build your life and career around your strengths; manage, don't obsess over, your weaknesses.

Feedback mastery — how elite performers operate
  • Hunt feedback actively — don't wait for it or avoid it
  • Seek immediate feedback wherever possible — build it into your workflow
  • Treat all feedback as data, not identity — detach ego from results
  • Distinguish feedback (information) from reassurance (emotional validation) — you want the former
